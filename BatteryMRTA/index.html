Abstract

Motivation

Multi-robot task allocation (MRTA) is essential for coordinating autonomous robot teams, and drone choreography offers a practical setting for assigning motion primitives among battery-constrained drones to achieve a synchronized performance.

Problem

Existing battery-aware MRTA methods struggle to combine accurate battery modeling with real-time allocation, often relying on simplified energy models or costly online replanning. This limitation is particularly critical in drone choreography, where battery prediction errors can disrupt synchronized primitive execution under tight energy margins.

Approach

To address this issue, we embed a differentiable battery simulation into the MRTA transition model, allowing maneuver-dependent energy dynamics to be modeled as part of the environment rather than through costly online optimization.

Method

We calibrate the simulation using flight data from Crazyflie nano-quadrotors executing representative motion primitives, yielding a data-consistent battery model for the target swarm platform. We integrate the model into a music-driven drone choreography pipeline and validate the system on motion primitive assignment and recharging decisions in both simulation and real-world experiments.

Results

The resulting framework is compatible with widely used decentralized MRTA algorithms and achieves higher mission success rates while reducing computation time compared with standard battery modeling baselines.
